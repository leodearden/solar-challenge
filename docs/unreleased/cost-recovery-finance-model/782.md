each home's window totals are scaled to the window's year by `k_h = year_days / days`, whatever the window's length, in the bills (§3) and in the projection (§4, *Annual basis*); `year_days` is 365 or 366 for a window within one calendar year and 365.25 for one that crosses a new year. The 360-day threshold is retired, and with it the rule the task 271 and 778 notes describe, so a window of 360 to 365 days is scaled too, and a 2024 window by 366/days, where the 0.5.0 tag's bills scale a window under 360 days by 365/days and any other by 1. The standing charge covers `year_days` days (§3): 366 in 2024, where the tag always bills 365. `householder_bill` no longer emits the tag's short-period `UserWarning`, nor `project_multi_year` the short-window one added since the tag. `householder_bill` and `bill_distribution` raise `ValueError` for a summary with no window, naming `calculate_summary`, and for a `simulation_days` other than their summaries' own, naming both; `householder_bill` for own-use below 0 or above the window's `total_demand_kwh`, naming both; and `bill_distribution` for summaries over different windows, as `project_multi_year` does for a fleet whose homes cover different windows, naming both windows (§3, §4). `BillDistribution` and `MultiYearCurve` carry `annualised_from`, the window their yearly figures were annualised from, None for `spreadsheet_revenue_curve`'s curve of yearly assumptions. §7.6's physics column, three June days of 2024, now scales by `k = 366/3 = 122`, not 365/3. When they re-pin, consumers re-check any bill, projection or solved rate of a window that is not one whole non-leap calendar year, give each summary they bill its window (`calculate_summary` sets it), pass the summaries' own `simulation_days`, and read `annualised_from` where they relied on the short-period warning to flag a short window.
