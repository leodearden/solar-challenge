With `self_consumption_override` set (the spreadsheet assumption), each projection year's fleet export and import (`YearPoint.fleet_export_kwh` and `fleet_import_kwh`, §4) are now the override's split, Σ_homes generation − own-use and demand − own-use, which is the export the year's SEG revenue is priced on. So for a fleet on one SEG rate, a year's SEG revenue ÷ its fleet export is that rate. The 0.5.0 tag reports the simulated export and import beside SEG revenue priced on the override's export. Each home's bill does not move, and nor do each simulated age's own-use and revenue: the bill and the projection now read one ledger per home (§3, *One ledger per home*), which assembles the same figures once. The projection's adaptive choice of which ages to simulate now measures the fleet export's and import's interpolation error against the age-0 fleet generation and demand (§4, *Node selection*), not against their own age-0 values. So a fleet that imports nothing at age 0, under the override with own-use capped at demand or on the simulated basis with a battery covering its demand, no longer simulates up to 12 ages and reports an `interp_error_estimate` of billions of percent. On either basis a projection can therefore simulate different ages: its interpolated years, its `interp_error_estimate` and the solved rate that reads them can move, within the error target, while each simulated age's own figures do not. When they re-pin, board readers re-check projections and the solved rates that read them.
