With `self_consumption_override` set (the spreadsheet assumption), each projection year's fleet export and import (`YearPoint.fleet_export_kwh` and `fleet_import_kwh`, §4) are now the override's split, Σ_homes generation − own-use and demand − own-use, which is the export the year's SEG revenue is priced on. So for a fleet on one SEG rate, a year's SEG revenue ÷ its fleet export is that rate. The 0.5.0 tag reports the simulated export and import beside SEG revenue priced on the override's export. Each home's bill does not move, and nor do each simulated age's own-use and revenue: the bill and the projection now read one ledger per home (§3, *One ledger per home*), which assembles the same figures once. The projection's adaptive choice of which ages to simulate reads the fleet export and import, which under the override are now the split's. For a fleet whose figures vary with age, its interpolated years, and the own-use rate the cost-recovery solve finds from them, can therefore move slightly. Physics-basis figures do not move beyond float precision. When they re-pin, board readers re-check spreadsheet-assumption projections and the solved rates that read them.
