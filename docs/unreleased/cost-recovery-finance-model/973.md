`finance run --assumptions` now drives the projection, the project economics and the cost-recovery solve from each mode's own `FinanceConfig`, as it already drove the bill (§3, *Override (spreadsheet-assumption) path*): `physics` clears any override, `spreadsheet` keeps the scenario's or sets 0.70, and `both` runs the two, one projection per mode. The 0.5.0 tag computes the economics and the solve once, on the scenario's `finance:` block whatever the mode, so its `physics` economics carry a scenario's YAML override and its `spreadsheet` economics ignore the 0.70 it bills at. Every table of the report is now one value column per mode, headed `Physics` or `Spreadsheet (<override>% self-consumption)` (§9); the '## Householder Bill (Physics Assumptions)' and '## Householder Bill — Physics vs Spreadsheet Assumptions' headings, and the single 'Value' column, go. The figures that move are those of a mode the scenario's finance block is not on. On `scenarios/bristol-fin-calibration.yaml`, whose block sets 0.70, `--assumptions physics` over 1-7 June 2024 now reports a mean fleet surplus of £50,328 a year, min DSCR 3.95×, equity IRR 11.3% and a CBS net surplus at the solved rate of £138.60 a home, where it reported the spreadsheet's £53,486, 4.20×, 11.9% and £117.92; its payback, 9.0 years, and its solved rate, clamped to 0.00 p/kWh, do not move. On `scenarios/bristol-phase1.yaml`, which sets no override, `--assumptions spreadsheet` over the same week now solves at 2.65 p/kWh, with a mean fleet surplus of £37,129, min DSCR 4.22×, equity IRR 11.9% and payback in 9.0 years, where it reported physics' 2.14 p/kWh, £28,804, 3.37×, 8.7% and 11.0 years. Readers of the report read each figure's mode from its column's header when they re-pin.
