A tariff whose periods share one rate has no cheap period, so time-of-use dispatch on it is self-consumption. The 0.5.0 tag classifies a minute as cheap when its rate is at or below the mean of the period rates, so every minute of a flat tariff (`TariffConfig.flat_rate(0.25)`, or `economy_7(off_peak_rate=0.25, peak_rate=0.25)`) is a cheap minute there; `simulate_timestep_tou` holds the battery through all of them and imports the whole shortfall. Task 182 changed that on `main`, and task 838 carries it on: a flat tariff's `cheap_minutes_of_day` is empty and `is_cheap_at` is `False` at every minute. A 2 kW shortfall for an hour against a fresh default 5 kWh battery (2.5 kWh stored) at 03:00 on a flat 0.25 £/kWh tariff discharges 0 kWh and imports 2.0 kWh (£0.50) on the tag, and discharges 1.95 kWh and imports 0.05 kWh (£0.0125) now, the result `simulate_timestep` returns with no tariff. The `GridChargeContext` that `simulate_timestep` builds from a tariff for a grid-charging battery changes with it: its `is_cheap_period` is `True` at every minute of a flat tariff on the tag and `False` at every minute now, so a grid-charging battery on a flat tariff no longer charges from the grid. Tariffs with a rate spread classify as before task 182, apart from what task 838's note describes. Consumers who run time-of-use dispatch or grid charging on a flat tariff re-baseline the figures built on them when they re-pin; other consumers need no change.
