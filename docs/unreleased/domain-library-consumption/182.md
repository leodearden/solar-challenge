A tariff whose periods share one rate has no cheap period, so time-of-use
dispatch on it is self-consumption. The 0.5.0 tag classifies a minute as cheap
when its rate is at or below the mean of the period rates, so every minute of a
flat tariff (`TariffConfig.flat_rate(0.25)`, or `economy_7(off_peak_rate=0.25,
peak_rate=0.25)`) is a cheap minute there; `simulate_timestep_tou` holds the
battery through all of them and imports the whole shortfall. Task 182 changed
that on `main`, and task 838 carries it on: a flat tariff's
`cheap_minutes_of_day` is empty and `is_cheap_at` is `False` at every minute. A
2 kW shortfall for an hour against a fresh default 5 kWh battery (2.5 kWh
stored) at 03:00 on a flat 0.25 £/kWh tariff discharges 0 kWh and imports 2.0
kWh (£0.50) on the tag, and discharges 1.95 kWh and imports 0.05 kWh (£0.0125)
now, the result `simulate_timestep` returns with no tariff. The
`GridChargeContext` that `simulate_timestep` builds from a tariff for a
grid-charging battery changes with it: its `is_cheap_period` is `True` at every
minute of a flat tariff on the tag and `False` at every minute now. Yet no
built-in dispatch charged from the grid on a flat tariff on the tag:
`compute_grid_charge_power_kw` refused a charge whose peak rate does not cover
the round-trip loss, which a flat tariff's peak rate, its current rate, never
does. So grid-charge figures do not move. What does change is the
`is_cheap_period` that a consumer's own `DispatchStrategy` receives in
`grid_charge_ctx`. Tariffs with a rate spread classify as before task 182, apart
from what task 838's note describes. Consumers who run time-of-use dispatch
(`simulate_timestep_tou`) on a flat tariff re-baseline the figures built on it
when they re-pin. Consumers whose own `DispatchStrategy` reads
`grid_charge_ctx.is_cheap_period` on a flat tariff check what it does now that
the flag is `False`. Other consumers need no change.
