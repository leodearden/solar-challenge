`Battery.discharge` never returns more than the
requested power, capped at `max_discharge_kw`, times the duration. The 0.5.0
tag returns the energy it withdraws times `discharge_efficiency`, which for
fewer than 1% of requests at the default 0.975 rounds one ulp above that
product; the state of charge still falls by the energy withdrawn. So a step's
`battery_discharge` from `simulate_timestep` or `simulate_timestep_tou`, and
`SummaryStatistics.total_battery_discharge_kwh` from `calculate_summary`, no
longer exceed the self-consumption they are part of, where the tag's can by an
ulp. `simulate_timestep_tou`'s expensive-period discharge now asks the battery
for `demand_kw - generation_kw`, as the dispatch strategies do, rather than the
step's shortfall energy divided by its duration, which on a step with both PV
and a shortfall can round an ulp away from it. So on a tariff with no rate
spread, a step with a shortfall gives exactly the results `simulate_timestep`
gives under its default self-consumption strategy; a step with surplus PV can
still differ from them in the last digit. Results can differ from the tag's in
the last digit; consumers need no change when they re-pin.
