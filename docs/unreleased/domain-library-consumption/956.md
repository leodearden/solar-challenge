`ScenarioConfig` gains three methods. `window()` is the
`timebase.SimulationWindow` of its period's first and last calendar days at
`timebase.ONE_MINUTE`, in the zone of its `location`, or of its first home's
location when `location` is None. `with_window(first_day=, last_day=)` is a
copy whose period keeps each day not given. `simulation_bounds()` is those
days' local midnights, the start and end a simulator takes. The 0.5.0 tag has
none of them. Construction raises `ConfigurationError`, a `ValueError`, for a
period whose `start_date` or `end_date` is not a date, a 'YYYY-MM-DD' string
or a `Timestamp`, naming `period.start_date` or `period.end_date` and the
value; the tag builds that scenario, and runs `SimulationPeriod("2024", "2024")`
as 2024-01-01. Construction also raises `ConfigurationError` for a period whose
last day precedes its first, naming both fields and both days, where the tag
builds the scenario. `with_window` and `dataclasses.replace` re-run both
checks. `project_multi_year`, `solve_cost_recovery_rate` and
`optimize.run_sweep` simulate over `simulation_bounds()`. So a period given as
a `Timestamp` with a time of day reaches `simulate` as its own date's local
midnight on the scenario's clock, where the tag passes the `Timestamp` as
given. A period of 'YYYY-MM-DD' strings or dates gets equal bounds.
`timebase` gains `DEFAULT_FIRST_DAY` and `DEFAULT_LAST_DAY`, 2024-01-01 and
2024-12-31: the days a scenario simulates when neither its file nor a flag
gives one. When they re-pin, consumers give a period's days as dates or
'YYYY-MM-DD' strings, and read a scenario's window from `window()` or
`simulation_bounds()` instead of `period.get_start_timestamp`.
